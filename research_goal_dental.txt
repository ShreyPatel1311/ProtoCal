Research goal: Propose a novel and genuinely feasible contribution at the intersection of artificial intelligence / machine learning and DENTAL BIOMATERIALS.

HARD CONSTRAINT - NO PATIENT-LEVEL VALIDATION.
The proposed work must be completable without any patient-level validation of any kind:
- no clinical trials, no human-subject recruitment, no IRB/ethics-board patient protocol;
- no patient records, no identifiable clinical images, no per-patient outcome data;
- validation must come only from in vitro / benchtop experiments, physics-based or
  molecular simulation, specimen-level (not patient-level) laboratory testing, and/or
  already-public datasets and published literature.
Any proposal whose central validation step requires patients is out of scope.

FEASIBILITY CONSTRAINT.
Executable by a single small academic lab in roughly 12 months, using standard
dental materials-science equipment (universal testing machine, microtensile/shear
bond-strength jigs, SEM, FTIR/Raman, XRD, profilometry, rheometer, thermocycler,
pH-cycling and artificial-saliva aging setups, standard microbiology for biofilm
assays) plus commodity compute (one or two GPUs). No new synchrotron time, no
proprietary industrial datasets, no multi-site consortium.

MATERIAL SCOPE (choose and justify a focus).
Resin composites and their fillers; dental adhesives and bonding agents; glass-ionomer
and bioactive/remineralizing cements; dental ceramics (zirconia, lithium disilicate);
denture base and 3D-printing photopolymer resins; implant surface coatings and
titanium surface treatments; antibacterial and regenerative/pulp-capping materials.

PROPERTIES OF INTEREST.
Mechanical (flexural/compressive strength, fracture toughness, modulus, wear),
adhesive (micro-tensile/shear bond strength, durability after thermocycling),
degradation and hydrolytic/enzymatic stability, polymerization shrinkage and
degree of conversion, water sorption/solubility, monomer elution and cytotoxicity
at the cell-culture level, ion release and remineralization capacity,
antibacterial/anti-biofilm performance, optical properties and color stability,
printability and dimensional accuracy for additive manufacturing.

WHAT A STRONG OUTPUT LOOKS LIKE.
Either (a) a novel AI/ML method that predicts, explains or inversely designs dental
biomaterial formulations and their specimen-level properties - ideally addressing the
real bottleneck in this field, which is small, heterogeneous, inconsistently reported
datasets - or (b) a new, carefully constructed benchmark dataset plus baseline models
that would let the community work on this problem at all. Methodological novelty must
be real (not "apply a standard CNN to an existing dataset"), the data source must be
concretely identified and actually obtainable, and the evaluation plan must be
specimen-level with honest baselines and ablations.
