# ProtoCal: Protocol-Aware Hierarchical Meta-Learning for Harmonizing Scattered Literature Data and Closed-Loop Inverse Design of Dental Resin Composites

## Proposed Method

## Material Focus and Justification
We focus on **dimethacrylate-based dental resin composites** (Bis-GMA/UDMA/TEGDMA matrices with glass/ceramic/nanofiller loadings). This class is chosen because (1) it is the most heavily studied dental biomaterial in the literature, with hundreds of papers systematically varying a small number of well-documented, numerically expressible formulation factors (monomer ratio, filler type/wt%/vol%/particle size, silanization, photoinitiator system, cure energy); (2) its core benchtop properties (flexural strength, degree of conversion, polymerization shrinkage, water sorption/solubility) are all governed by ISO 4049 and measurable with equipment present in any standard dental materials lab; and (3) formulations are genuinely combinatorial, making inverse design scientifically meaningful (there is a real, high-dimensional space to search) rather than a toy relabeling problem.

## The Core Problem We Target
The field's real bottleneck is not lack of data volume but **uncontrolled heterogeneity**: published studies differ in specimen geometry, storage medium/duration, thermocycling regime, testing-machine crosshead speed, and especially **curing protocol** (LED vs. QTH, irradiance, distance, exposure time). These nuisance factors are often partially reported and are entangled with the composition signal, so naive pooling of literature data (the standard practice in essentially all existing dental-composite ML papers, which fit a CNN/ANN/random forest directly on concatenated multi-study tables and validate with ordinary k-fold CV) produces models that fit idiosyncratic study-specific noise rather than the true composition→property relationship, and that cannot be trusted for extrapolative, inverse-design use.

## Step 1: Structured Literature Dataset (DentalCompDB)
We construct and publicly release a schema-driven dataset distinguishing two feature blocks for every extracted record:
- **Composition/process vector x** (the variable we want the model to learn from): monomer identity and mass/mol ratios (optionally encoded via RDKit-computed descriptors — molecular weight, C=C double-bond density, computed viscosity proxy — so the model can generalize to monomers not literally seen before, not just memorize one-hot IDs); filler type, wt%/vol%, mean particle size and distribution class (nano/micro/hybrid), silanization (yes/no, type); photoinitiator system and concentration; and a single physically meaningful **cure-dose feature** (irradiance × time = radiant exposure, J/cm²).
- **Protocol/nuisance vector p** (treated as a conditioning context, not as a feature to be creatively exploited): specimen geometry/standard (ISO 4049 vs. ADA vs. custom), storage medium and duration pre-test, thermocycling count, crosshead speed, temperature.
Each record also carries provenance (DOI, table/figure id) for auditability.

**Extraction quality control (upgraded).** Extraction uses PDF table-parsing tools plus LLM-assisted structured extraction. Because downstream statistical conclusions (especially the b_s/ε_s data-quality signal in Step 2) are only as trustworthy as the extraction itself, we move beyond a single audit pass: (a) every record is extracted independently by two LLM passes with different prompting strategies plus one rule-based parser where tables are machine-readable; disagreements are flagged automatically; (b) a stratified 35% human-verification sample (oversampling flagged-disagreement records, shrinkage-measurement-method records, and non-English/figure-only sources, which are the highest-error categories) is manually adjudicated by two independent raters; (c) we report Cohen's κ for categorical fields and mean relative error for numeric fields, broken out by extraction-difficulty stratum, not just as a single pooled number. This is still an explicit, rarely-reported data-quality metric for this field, but with a defensible sampling design.

**Missing-data handling.** Not every paper reports all four target properties for every formulation (e.g., shrinkage is reported less often than flexural strength), and this missingness is plausibly not random (labs may selectively report favorable or easy-to-measure properties). We treat the dataset as a long-format table (one row per formulation×property×study) rather than requiring complete rows, so training uses every observed value without imputation. We additionally test the missing-completely-at-random assumption by regressing property-reporting-rate on study-level covariates (sample size, publication venue, year) and report this as an explicit limitation/robustness check rather than assuming it away.

## Step 2: Hierarchical Protocol-Conditioned Meta-Model (ProtoCal)
For a property value y observed for formulation i in study s, we model:

y_{i,s} = f_shared(x_i; θ) + g_proto(p_s; φ) + b_s + ε_s

- **f_shared** is a shared composition→property function (we compare a multi-output neural network — jointly predicting flexural strength, DC%, shrinkage, and water sorption/solubility, since these are mechanistically correlated and joint training improves sample efficiency on small per-study n — against a multi-output Gaussian Process with a physically structured kernel over x, which gives calibrated epistemic uncertainty needed for the design loop).
- **g_proto** is a learned, *structured* correction term that takes the protocol vector p_s (not just a study ID) through a small embedding network. Unlike standard random-intercept/one-hot mixed-effects models used in classic meta-analysis (which cannot generalize beyond protocol combinations already observed), g_proto generalizes zero-shot to new protocol combinations by composing learned effects of geometry, aging, and cure dose.
- **b_s** is a per-study random residual offset and **ε_s** a per-study heteroscedastic noise term, both partially pooled (empirical-Bayes shrinkage) toward zero/global variance.

**Identifiability safeguards (addressing the central statistical risk in this design).** Because every study typically reports under one fixed protocol, p_s is constant within a study, so g_proto(p_s) and b_s are both per-study additive constants and are not separable from any single study in isolation — this must be solved structurally, not assumed away:
1. *Gauge fixing.* We define g_proto(p_reference) ≡ 0 by construction (p_reference = the ISO 4049 reference protocol used for our in-house anchors). This removes the additive ambiguity between f_shared's implicit offset, g_proto, and b_s, and makes f_shared directly interpretable as the reference-protocol prediction.
2. *Asymmetric regularization.* g_proto is a low-capacity, shared function (small embedding dimension, weight decay, and where physically justified, monotonicity constraints — e.g., cure dose effect on DC% is constrained non-decreasing) fit jointly across all studies, whereas b_s is an independent free scalar per study under a strong zero-mean shrinkage prior whose variance τ² is itself learned via empirical Bayes/marginal likelihood. This encodes the correct inductive bias: any between-study variation that correlates with observed protocol features should be explained by the shared g_proto; b_s should only absorb the residual, genuinely unexplainable study-specific idiosyncrasy. Identification is statistically analogous to separating a fixed effect (shared, protocol-indexed, continuous-covariate function) from random intercepts in a mixed model, and is viable because protocol variables (irradiance, time, thermocycling count, storage duration) vary continuously and overlap across many study pairs rather than being study-unique categorical labels.
3. *Synthetic-recovery validation (new, run before trusting the real-data decomposition).* We generate synthetic data from a known ground-truth f_shared, g_proto, and b_s with realistic missingness and study-size imbalance mirroring DentalCompDB, fit the model, and report recovery error (correlation and RMSE between true and fitted g_proto, b_s) as a precondition check. If recovery is poor under realistic sparsity, we reduce g_proto capacity and/or increase the number of distinct protocol combinations required per shared-dimension bin before trusting literature-derived harmonization — this gating step is pre-registered as part of the modeling pipeline, not an optional diagnostic.
4. *Post-hoc diagnostic.* After fitting on real data, we regress the fitted b_s values on p_s; a near-zero relationship confirms g_proto has absorbed the explainable protocol signal rather than leaving it to leak into b_s (and by extension into the data-quality-signal interpretation of b_s described below).

With these safeguards, the fitted b_s/ε_s values remain an interpretable by-product: a data-quality signal correlating with sample size and standard-compliance that flags outlier/likely-erroneous studies in the field, now with a documented basis for why that signal is not simply absorbing protocol confounds.

Training jointly maximizes the marginal likelihood (GP variant) or ELBO/NLL (variational NN variant) over all literature records.

## Step 3: Anchor Calibration with In-House Gold-Standard Data
A designed set of ~50 formulations (Latin hypercube over filler vol%, monomer ratio, and fixed, single reference cure protocol) is fabricated and tested in-house under one controlled protocol. These **anchor points** are added to the training objective with p_s fixed to p_reference (the same protocol used for gauge-fixing g_proto above); they pin down the absolute scale of f_shared (correcting literature-wide publication-bias/optimistic-reporting drift) and let us validate that g_proto-corrected literature predictions agree with real reference-protocol measurements — directly testing the harmonization hypothesis rather than assuming it. The anchor sample size (~50 over 3 design dimensions, with an option to extend sequentially — see Experiment Plan) is chosen to balance statistical power for the Step-2 calibration against realistic benchtop throughput for a single lab.

## Step 4: Closed-Loop Inverse Design
Using the calibrated model's predictive mean and uncertainty, we run multi-objective Bayesian optimization (noisy expected hypervolume improvement, qNEHVI) over the formulation space x to propose Pareto-optimal candidates jointly maximizing flexural strength and degree of conversion while minimizing shrinkage and water sorption/solubility. The BO loop uses a fixed reference point (set at the 10th percentile of each objective's observed anchor-set range, worse-than-observed in every dimension) for hypervolume computation, proposes candidates in batches of 5 (q=5) per round to match realistic benchtop batch-fabrication capacity, and runs for 2–3 rounds totaling 10–15 physical formulations. Top-ranked candidates are fabricated and benchtop-tested (new specimens, same reference protocol), closing the loop with genuine prospective validation — the form of evidence almost entirely absent from existing dental-materials ML papers, which validate only by resampling the same literature table.

**Why this should work:** the method directly targets the stated bottleneck (heterogeneous, inconsistently reported data) with a mechanism — structured protocol conditioning plus partial pooling plus a small controlled anchor set — rather than ignoring it (pooling) or only removing it adversarially (domain-invariant learning, which would also destroy real composition-aging interactions we may want to predict, e.g., shrinkage under different cure doses). Unlike the original formulation, the revised model makes the identifiability conditions for separating protocol effects from study noise explicit and empirically checked, rather than implicit and assumed.

## Experiment Plan

## Datasets
1. **DentalCompDB (new, literature-derived):** Systematic search (PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science) 2000–2025 for studies on experimental or commercial dimethacrylate dental composites reporting flexural strength (ISO 4049 3-/4-point bending), degree of conversion (FTIR/Raman), polymerization shrinkage (strain gauge, bonded-disk, AccuVol, or density-based methods), and/or water sorption/solubility (ISO 4049 gravimetric), with explicit composition tables (own-formulated composites, which report filler/monomer ratios explicitly, are prioritized). Target: 150–300 papers, 800–1500 formulation×property records, each annotated with the composition vector x and protocol vector p defined above, plus DOI provenance. Extraction uses the dual-LLM-pass + rule-based-parser pipeline with a stratified 35% human-adjudicated audit (see Proposed Method, Step 1), reporting κ/MRE by stratum. Released publicly (Zenodo/Figshare) with schema documentation and the extraction-agreement report — this is itself a community benchmark contribution.
2. **In-house anchor/validation set (new, benchtop):** ~50 formulations from a Latin hypercube design over filler vol% (40–70%, silanized barium-aluminosilicate glass), Bis-GMA:TEGDMA(:UDMA) ratio, and camphorquinone/amine concentration, all cured under one fixed reference protocol (standard LED, 1200 mW/cm², 20 s). Sample-size justification: with 3 active design dimensions, 50 points gives roughly 16–17 points per dimension equivalent under space-filling design, in line with common rule-of-thumb guidance (~10× dimensionality) for surrogate-model calibration at this scale; we treat this as a floor and specify a sequential-augmentation option (+15 formulations added if the Step-2 calibration diagnostics in the ablation below show under-identification) rather than treating 50 as fixed and unquestionable. Each formulation: n=5 bars (25×2×2 mm) for 3-point flexure on a universal testing machine; FTIR-ATR real-time cure monitoring for DC%; bonded-disk/strain-gauge shrinkage measurement; ISO 4049 gravimetric disks (15×1 mm) for water sorption/solubility. Split into (a) training anchors used for calibration and (b) a held-out prospective test subset of formulations never seen in DentalCompDB. We additionally carve out a **held-out-monomer split**: a small number of anchor formulations use a monomer ratio/filler combination deliberately absent from any DentalCompDB record, specifically to test whether the RDKit-descriptor encoding (vs. categorical encoding) extrapolates to compositions outside the literature's observed support.
3. **Round-2 BO validation batch:** 10–15 freshly fabricated formulations proposed by the trained model's Bayesian-optimization loop (batches of 5, 2–3 rounds, fixed hypervolume reference point — see Proposed Method, Step 4), tested identically, used only for final prospective evaluation (never touched during model fitting).

**Feasibility and resourcing.** Estimated timeline: ~5 months literature search/extraction/QC (Step 1, can run in parallel with tooling development), ~3 months anchor fabrication and benchtop testing (Step 3, standard dental-materials-lab equipment: universal testing machine, FTIR-ATR, LED curing unit, thermocycler, analytical balance), ~2 months model development/ablations (Step 2), ~2 months BO loop plus Round-2 fabrication/testing (Step 4). Materials cost for ~65 total formulations (anchors + Round-2) at small-batch benchtop scale is modest relative to standard dental-materials-lab budgets and uses equipment already specified as present in any standard dental materials lab (per Material Focus justification).

## Baselines
- **Pooled-ML baseline:** multi-output ANN/random forest trained on all literature records with composition features only, ignoring study/protocol identity — representative of current published practice in dental-composite ML papers.
- **Standard mixed-effects model:** linear/ANN model with per-study one-hot random intercepts (classic meta-analytic approach; cannot generalize to unseen protocol combinations).
- **Domain-adversarial NN (DANN):** representation trained to be invariant to study identity, as a contrasting domain-generalization strategy.
- **Naive transfer learning:** pretrain on pooled literature, fine-tune only on anchor set.
- **Single global GP:** one Matérn-kernel GP over composition features with no hierarchical/protocol structure.
- **Random-search and single-objective greedy** baselines for the inverse-design/BO stage.
All model-comparison metrics below are reported with uncertainty: we use 10 random seeds per model/split combination and report bootstrap 95% confidence intervals, with paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (across CV folds) for head-to-head claims (e.g., ProtoCal vs. pooled-ML), rather than single-run point estimates.

## Metrics
- **Predictive accuracy** (RMSE/MAE per property: MPa for flexural strength, % for DC and shrinkage, µg/mm³ for sorption/solubility) under (i) random k-fold split, (ii) leave-one-study-out CV, (iii) **leave-one-lab-out CV** (grouping studies by research group/affiliation rather than by paper, since multiple papers from the same lab often share undocumented protocol idiosyncrasies that leave-one-study-out alone would not catch — a harder and more realistic generalization test), and (iv) the held-out in-house prospective test set (the most important number, since it is true generalization beyond anything used in fitting) plus the held-out-monomer split specifically for the chemistry-informed-encoding ablation.
- **Uncertainty calibration:** negative log-likelihood and prediction-interval coverage probability (PICP) at 90%/95% on the prospective test set.
- **Harmonization quality:** for near-duplicate compositions tested across ≥2 different studies, measure variance of raw reported values vs. variance after g_proto-based protocol correction (report % variance reduction).
- **Identifiability validation:** synthetic-data recovery error for g_proto and b_s (correlation and RMSE vs. ground truth, see Proposed Method Step 2 safeguard 3); post-hoc correlation between fitted b_s and observed p_s on real data (should be near zero, confirming no leakage).
- **Data-quality signal validity:** correlation between fitted per-study residual/noise terms (b_s, ε_s) and independent proxies of study reliability (reported sample size, standard compliance, journal metrics).
- **Inverse-design performance:** Pareto hypervolume (fixed reference point as defined in Step 4) achieved vs. number of physical benchtop experiments consumed (sample efficiency) relative to random/greedy search; measured-vs-predicted agreement (RMSE, calibration) for the physically fabricated Round-2 BO candidates — the ultimate real-world test of the whole pipeline.

## Ablation Studies
1. **Remove protocol embedding** (plain per-study random intercept instead of structured g_proto(p_s)) — isolates the benefit of generalizable protocol conditioning versus classic mixed-effects modeling.
2. **Remove anchor calibration** (train on literature only, zero-shot predict the reference protocol) — quantifies the value of the small controlled in-house dataset.
3. **Anchor-set size sweep** (10/20/40/50/60+15 formulations) — produces a sample-efficiency curve answering how much benchtop data is actually needed, directly relevant to adoption by other small labs, and empirically justifies (or revises) the n=50 default via the sequential-augmentation trigger described above.
4. **Multi-task vs. single-task heads** (joint 4-property prediction vs. four independent models) — tests whether exploiting cross-property correlation helps under data scarcity.
5. **GP vs. neural-network f_shared** — accuracy/calibration trade-off, and which backbone is more reliable inside the BO loop.
6. **Chemistry-informed (RDKit) vs. categorical monomer encoding** — tests extrapolation to monomer combinations absent from the literature set, evaluated specifically on the held-out-monomer anchor split.
7. **With vs. without per-study heteroscedastic noise weighting** — shows whether automatic downweighting of unreliable studies improves held-out accuracy versus equal-weighting all literature records.
8. **Identifiability/gauge-fixing ablation (new):** compare (a) full model with gauge-fixing + asymmetric shrinkage, (b) gauge-fixing removed, (c) shrinkage-prior strength on b_s swept across a range, reporting synthetic-recovery error and real-data b_s–p_s leakage diagnostic for each configuration — directly demonstrates that the identifiability safeguards in Step 2 are necessary and quantifies sensitivity to the shrinkage hyperparameter rather than treating it as a fixed, unexamined choice.
